Simulation software

Chronergy126In development

One platform for everything a race engineer does on a weekend

On the pit wall I kept jumping between spreadsheets, the logger software, a sim tool and a dozen chat threads. Chronergy126 puts it in one web app: run plan, tyre allocation, lap time analysis, setup history, simulation and stint reports.

I have worked on Chronergy126 for about two years. I designed the interface in Figma and worked with an external team of six developers and two project managers, running the daily meetings, reviewing the user stories and accepting each delivery. Today I run the project on my own: development, project management and the engineering behind it.

The main technical problem was simulation throughput. One run takes about 30 seconds, so we spread the batches over several Azure machines in parallel. That makes a 200-run study usable during a race weekend, not just between events.

16modules
300+screens designed in Figma
~25 minfor 200 simulations on 4 cloud machines

The vehicle model behind it covers multibody transient dynamics, a thermal tyre model fitted to on-track data, a virtual 7-post rig, and track bump profiles rebuilt from logged car data.

Python automation

Stint report

From two hours of clicking to five minutes, twice a day

After each stint, the data engineer exported the logger data, clicked through the channels one tab at a time, checked them against limits they kept in their head, and printed graphs one by one. It took up to two hours per report, and there was no guarantee every graph made it in.

I replaced it with a Python script. You pick the session file and it does the rest: reads the MoTeC or WinTAX export, computes the math channels and KPIs, flags anything outside its limits, draws every plot in the same style, builds the PDF and sends it to the team's WhatsApp group.

The engineers get the same report every time, and I get the time back for driver and setup work.

~2 hper report by hand
~5 minwith the script
  1. Export the logger data
  2. Pick the session file
  3. Channels, KPIs, limit checks
  4. Plots and PDF
  5. Sent to WhatsApp

Side project · Web app, iOS app in progress

Spindle.fm

A vinyl collection app that feels like your living room

Every record collection app I tried was a list. I wanted mine to feel like standing in front of my shelves. In Spindle your records live on a teak record wall in a warm 3D living room, one cubby per genre, and every feature is an object in that room.

The record wall
Swipe through the cubbies. Tap one and its crate slides out like a drawer so you can flip through the sleeves.
The turntable
Drop the needle and hear a 30-second preview.
The camera
Add a record by scanning its barcode (exact pressing and market value from Discogs) or by photographing the sleeves.
The phone
Call a friend and visit their room to browse their shelves.
The radio
Random previews by station and genre.
The notebook
Your want list, which warns you before you buy a record twice.
The mailbox
Lend or pass records on to friends.
